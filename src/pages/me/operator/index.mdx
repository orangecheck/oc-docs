# ochk.app · Operator Program

**Run a guardian. Earn sats. Hold the key.**

In the design, an orangecheck federation is run by independent **guardians**:
one operator key per guardian, each guardian threshold-signing federation
actions with its peers. orangecheck recruits, vets and pays guardians, and every
commitment a guardian makes is signed with its own operator key.

**The program is not open.** While the system is in testing, orangecheck holds
every key that can move funds, and there are no guardian seats yet.
[ochk.app/custody](https://ochk.app/custody) shows who holds what today. The
operator pages on ochk.app open only to accounts the program lets in; to anyone
else the operator routes on me.ochk.io answer `403`. This page describes how the
program works.

Two paths into it:

- **[The portal at ochk.app](https://ochk.app/me/operator)** — sign in, make or
  import an operator key, fill the application form, sign and submit in the
  browser. The lifecycle (key custody, application, charter ratification,
  ceremonies, incident publishing) runs in the portal without a CLI.
- **[The kit](https://github.com/orangecheck/oc-guardian-kit)** —
  `oc-guardian init` on a server you control, fill the questionnaire, run
  `oc-guardian apply prepare`, email `application.json` to apply@ochk.io. Each
  portal step has a kit equivalent; the envelopes are the same, and so is the
  review queue.

## Lifecycle

| Stage           | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Where                                                                      |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **identity**    | Generate (or import) an Ed25519 operator keypair. The browser stores it in IndexedDB, backed up by a 24-word BIP-39 phrase; the kit stores it in the OS keychain, or in a key file (`--key-file`) on a server.      | [ochk.app/me/operator](https://ochk.app/me/operator)                       |
| **application** | Fill the questionnaire (handle, contact, hardware setup, motivation, etc.). `oc-guardian apply prepare` hashes the answers and signs a `program-apply` envelope into `application.json`; email it to apply@ochk.io. | Kit + email                                                                |
| **review**      | OC team verifies the envelope signature, reads the questionnaire, and decides. Reviewer signs an `program-accept` envelope on accept; you verify it locally before trusting.                                        | Reviewer side                                                              |
| **federation**  | Once you are accepted, an orangecheck admin assigns you a guardian seat, and you ratify the federation's charter: a `charter-sign` envelope under your operator key. The ratification is your join.                 | [ochk.app/me/operator/ceremonies](https://ochk.app/me/operator/ceremonies) |
| **operate**     | Run the `oc-guardian` binary on hardware you control or hardware OC provisions on your behalf. Publish incidents (`alert-publish`), participate in ceremonies (`charter-sign`), claim payouts (`payouts-claim`).    | Portal + binary                                                            |

## Two ways to run the guardian binary

The federation cannot distinguish between these. Pick whichever fits.

### Option A · bring your own hardware

You own the host. Pull the cosign-verified Docker image and run
`oc-guardian fedimintd run`. Its settings come from the environment: your
operator id and pubkey, the federation, the bitcoin network, a chain source and
the public host name. Peers reach it on port 9000 (P2P) and clients on port 9001
(API). Full runbook: [/me/operator/runbook](/me/operator/runbook).

### Option B · let OC provision for you

Opt into hosted hardware on the dashboard. orangecheck runs the signed image on
one hosting provider, in a region you choose, and the portal can restart, stop
or destroy the machine. Your operator key never touches the host: charter
ratifications, payout claims and exits are still signed in your browser or your
kit. The guardian's `fedimintd` does hold its own key share on that machine, and
it signs liveness reports with a key orangecheck made, which counts for your
seat only while you delegate to it. A hosted guardian is only as independent as
orangecheck, which runs the machine. Where that matters, run it on hardware you
control.

Pricing in sats, billed against your operator payouts. Decommission any time.

## Architectural invariants

These hold for every federation in the program. Anything that violates them is a
bug.

1. **Operator-held keys.** OC the company has zero credentials capable of
   signing as you. Browser IDB or kit OS keychain are equivalent storage — both
   are operator-controlled.
2. **Bypass parity.** Every portal feature has a documented kit-only equivalent
   in
   [BYPASS.md](https://github.com/orangecheck/oc-guardian-kit/blob/main/BYPASS.md).
   A guardian operating purely via the kit is architecturally indistinguishable
   from one operating with the portal.
3. **Public commitments.** Charter ratifications, incidents, attestations are
   operator-signed and publicly verifiable offline. OC cannot forge a row in any
   of these timelines.
4. **Funds sit with the federation.** In a federation, the guardians hold the
   bitcoin collectively, and orangecheck's executor holds each user's claim on
   it, as ecash it can spend, until it pays them out
   ([ochk.app/custody](https://ochk.app/custody)). Today there are no guardian
   seats: while the system is in testing, orangecheck holds every key that can
   move funds, and the sats users earn.
