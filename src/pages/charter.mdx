# Charter

**Ratified v1 · 2026-04-28.**

A commercial entity built on top of an open protocol family has obligations the
protocol layer cannot enforce on its own. These are the ones OrangeCheck accepts
publicly. The full text is committed to git so the diff is auditable; this page
is the canonical published version.

The charter binds the **whole company**. Every product that ships under the
OrangeCheck name — `ochk.app`, `vault.ochk.io`, and every sibling protocol site
(`attest`, `lock`, `vote`, `stamp`, `agent`, `pledge`) — operates under these
eight commitments. There is one charter, in one place, at ochk.io/docs. Product
subdomains link here.

## The eight commitments

### 01 · No token. Ever.

OrangeCheck does not and will not issue a token, points balance, airdrop, or any
unit of account other than sats and USD. The economic loop closes on Bitcoin.
Anyone proposing otherwise is not OrangeCheck.

### 02 · No custody you didn't choose.

OrangeCheck does not hold customer Bitcoin private keys and does not operate a
custodial wallet you cannot move out of. On the protocol siblings, no custody —
period. Bonded reputation is attestation-of-unspent: sats stay in the customer's
own wallet, enforcement is by exposure, never by slashing.

`ochk.app` is the one place this commitment has a stated exception, and it is a
stage rather than a design. **While it is in testing, OrangeCheck holds what a
user accrues there and is the party that moves it**, because it is operating
every role in the system until independent operators take them. The design is
federation custody — an M-of-N guardian set, OC holding no share — with a
one-click move to your own keys that keeps the same address and the same
history. Neither is in force yet: no federation is bound, which is
machine-readable at [`/api/federations`](https://me.ochk.io/api/federations) and
stated in the present tense at [ochk.app/custody](https://ochk.app/custody). We
say that here rather than in a footnote, because a charter that overstates is
worth less than one that names its own exception.

### 03 · The protocol stays open.

The wire format, scope grammar, audit-bundle structure, and Bitcoin-anchoring
rules are defined by the open `oc-*-protocol` specs (`oc-attest-protocol`,
`oc-lock-protocol`, `oc-vote-protocol`, `oc-stamp-protocol`,
`oc-agent-protocol`, `oc-pledge-protocol`). Every change OrangeCheck makes lands
as a public commit in that spec's repository, and each spec keeps a changelog.
No private extensions, no closed conformance vectors.

### 04 · The protocol sites stay free.

`attest.ochk.io`, `lock.ochk.io`, `vote.ochk.io`, `stamp.ochk.io`,
`agent.ochk.io`, `pledge.ochk.io` — every sibling protocol site remains free,
self-hostable, no-account public goods forever. The consumer commercial products
(`ochk.app`, `vault.ochk.io`) are optional layers, never the only path to using
the protocols. If we ever change this, the spec stewardship governance ratifies
first.

### 05 · Audit verifies without us.

Every audit bundle and every receipt OrangeCheck issues, billable or not, is
verifiable offline, against Bitcoin headers, by anyone, forever. If OrangeCheck
disappeared tomorrow, every receipt continues to verify. We sell operations, not
lock-in. The protocol is the API.

### 06 · No agent-replacing-humans positioning.

OrangeCheck sells authority infrastructure. We do not market agents as labor
displacement. The pitch is that compliance teams can sign off on agents taking
real authority — which is human work being elevated, not erased.

### 07 · Sat-denominated economics, first-class.

Sat-paid customers are not a footnote. Every USD price is published with its sat
equivalent on the same row. We do not require a fiat onramp and do not treat
sat-paid customers as a niche segment. Payments to OrangeCheck are made
on-chain: Lightning has been off across the family since 2026-09-02. On
`ochk.app` specifically, every billable event has its price, OC's platform fee
and what the user earns published in sats — USD shown alongside, never as the
canonical unit.

### 08 · Spec stewardship is public.

Material changes to the spec stewardship structure are announced publicly and
discussed in the `oc-protocol` governance forum before being adopted.
OrangeCheck has one seat at that table; it is not the chair. Material changes to
**this charter** itself follow the same path: a published proposal, a public
review window, and a versioned re-ratification.

## How this applies per product

- **`ochk.app`** — consumer commercial product (its API is `me.ochk.io`).
  OrangeCheck-held while in testing; federation custody, then your own keys, is
  the destination (commitment 02). Three kinds of billable event: one-time, per
  action and per session. Integrators set what each event pays the user; OC's
  fee is a fixed 20% of the price. Commitments **02** (custody you can move out
  of), **05** (every receipt verifies offline), and **07** (sats first-class)
  bind this product specifically.
- **`vault.ochk.io`** — consumer commercial product. Bitcoin-bound encrypted
  secrets storage built on OC Lock Flow 4. OC holds ciphertext only — no entry
  name, type, or payload is ever readable server-side, and there is no recovery
  backdoor by construction. Commitments **02** (no custody — the vault key never
  leaves the user's device, OC cannot decrypt), **03/04** (`oc-lock-protocol` is
  open and free forever), **05** (every entry exports as a portable artifact any
  holder of the key can decrypt offline via `@orangecheck/lock-crypto`), and
  **07** (paid in sats on-chain, into a watch-only wallet, no fiat) bind this
  product specifically.
- **Protocol siblings** — every `oc-*-protocol` spec repo + the matching
  `*.ochk.io` reference site. Commitments **03** (open spec), **04** (free
  forever), and **05** (offline-verifiable) bind these specifically.

## If we ever break one of these

The protocol layer continues to work without us. Every receipt we ever issued
continues to verify. The spec repos stay public under the MIT license their
`LICENSE` files state, and the npm packages remain published. Customers can fork
the managed components and run them themselves on day one. This is the whole
point of the design.

Spec stewardship and family governance live at
[github.com/orangecheck](https://github.com/orangecheck). Every change to a
spec, and to this page, is a public commit there.
