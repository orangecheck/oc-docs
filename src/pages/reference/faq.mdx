# FAQ

Common questions across all the sibling protocols.

## Do coins move?

Not for a proof. No protocol broadcasts Bitcoin transactions: every protocol
signs messages, and chain state is **read, not written**. Your funds never leave
your wallet to make a proof.

ochk.app is different. While it is in testing, orangecheck holds the sats apps
pay you there and is the party that moves them.
[ochk.app/custody](https://ochk.app/custody) says who holds what, read live.

## Is this custodial?

The protocols are not. No protocol and no reference implementation requires a
third party to hold funds, keys, or signatures. The signed message + signature
is the artifact; you share it directly.

ochk.app is the exception while it is in testing. orangecheck holds what you
earn there, and holds the sign-in key of an account made with email, Google or
GitHub. No federation is bound yet; see
[ochk.app/custody](https://ochk.app/custody) and the [charter](/charter).

## Which wallets work?

Anything that signs a message with BIP-322, or with the legacy `signmessage`
format (BIP-137) using the address's own key. BIP-322 is preferred; the legacy
format is accepted for every address type too.

- **Browser:** UniSat, Xverse, Leather, OKX, Phantom. Not Alby: WebLN signs with
  a Lightning node key, not a Bitcoin address.
- **Desktop:** Sparrow, Bitcoin Core, Electrum (through its legacy `signmessage`
  signature)
- **Hardware:** via PSBT flow — Coldcard, Trezor (with limitations), Ledger
  (limited)

See the [BIP-322 signing](/ecosystem/bip322) page for the full matrix.

## Is OC Attest reputation?

No. Reputation implies social judgment. OC Attest is a cryptographic stake
receipt — it tells a verifier "this address holds N sats and has held them for N
days." That's a cost signal, not a reputation score.

Calling it reputation misleads users into expecting social-graph-style trust
transitivity that isn't there.

## Is OC Attest identity / KYC?

No. An attestation proves address control plus some self-asserted handles.
That's not proof-of-human, not proof-of-name, not KYC. See
[compared to alternatives](/getting-started/which-protocol) for the full matrix.

## What prevents forging a proof?

Three layers:

1. **Bitcoin signatures are cryptographically unforgeable.** BIP-322
   verification is deterministic public-key math.
2. **Chain state is publicly auditable.** `sats_bonded` and `days_unspent` are
   recomputed from mempool.space / blockstream.info / your own node on every
   verify.
3. **Identity bindings are self-asserted.** Verifiers MUST check handle
   ownership out-of-band; nothing in the protocol is "trust the claim."

## What if I spend my bonded UTXOs?

The next verify sees `bond_zero` or `bond_insufficient`. That's the revocation
path — the chain is the authority.

## Can I use a fresh address each time?

Yes, and for privacy-conscious use cases you SHOULD. Publishing a proof
permanently links the address to the handles you assert. A fresh single-purpose
address avoids that linkage for your main stack.

## Can a whale game stake-weighted systems?

Yes, at proportional cost. Holding `N × min_sats` lets an attacker produce `N`
passing sybils. Raise thresholds to raise the cost. Pair with non-economic
signals (humanness proofs, graph inputs) when one-per-human matters.

See
[security model — sybil at the economic floor](/ecosystem/security#threats-to-the-verifier).

## Why Bitcoin and not Ethereum?

Bitcoin's opportunity cost is real, measurable, and adversary-agnostic. Ethereum
gas is volatile; most non-Bitcoin stake proofs involve governance tokens that
can be inflated or forked. Bitcoin UTXOs give the cleanest economic signal on
the open internet.

Design rule: _"if a feature works identically on Ed25519, it doesn't belong
here."_ The Bitcoin economic layer is load-bearing.

## How is OC Attest different from Worldcoin / BrightID?

Worldcoin and BrightID are **proof-of-personhood** protocols — they try to
answer "is this one unique human?" OC Attest is a **cost filter** — "does this
address have Bitcoin skin in the game?" Different questions, different
solutions. They compose well: OC Attest at the entry gate, personhood system
above it.

## How is OC Attest different from NIP-05?

NIP-05 proves control of a DNS domain via an HTTP lookup. OC Attest proves
control of a Bitcoin address via a cryptographic signature. Both are
self-asserted identity primitives for Nostr; Attest adds an economic signal that
NIP-05 doesn't have.

Use NIP-05 when "which domain is this" matters. Use OC Attest when "has this
person committed real capital" matters. Stack both for either signal alone.

## Do I have to publish to Nostr?

No — publishing is optional. The attestation envelope is a self-contained JSON
blob that verifies offline. Publishing just makes it discoverable by address,
handle, or ID without the caller needing to already have the blob. Same for Lock
envelopes, Stamp envelopes, and Vote ballots.

## Can I self-host?

Yes. Every protocol's reference implementation is MIT-licensed and runs on your
infrastructure — no hosted dependency. For OC Attest in particular,
`@orangecheck/sdk` is a complete local verifier (Nostr query + BIP-322 check +
Esplora fetch) and needs nothing from ochk.io.

The hosted `ochk.io/api/*` endpoints exist for convenience; none of the
protocols require them.

## What about privacy-preserving proofs (ZK)?

Out of scope for v0 of every protocol. The address is public by design so any
verifier can recompute. For privacy, rotate addresses — use a fresh,
single-purpose address per proof.

ZK-style private-balance proofs are future research. The current family
prioritizes offline-verifiability and transparency over privacy.

## Why is the protocol header frozen at v0?

Any change to the canonical message format — including adding a version number —
breaks every existing signature. OC Attest's header is the literal
`orangecheck`, with no version number, on purpose: a new format needs a
different header (e.g., `orangecheck-v1`) and would coexist with this one. The
other protocols carry a version in their header: `oc-stamp:v1`,
`oc-agent:delegation:v1`, `oc-pledge/v1`, `oc-lock:device-bind:v3`, and
`oc-vote/v0/commit` for a vote commit. See
[canonical message](/ecosystem/canonical-message).

## What's the difference between OC Stamp and OC Pledge?

Both produce BIP-322-signed envelopes anchored to Bitcoin. The difference is
**when the truth lives**:

- **Stamp** says "this content existed, signed by me, before this Bitcoin
  block." The truth is _backward-looking_ — the bytes are already final.
- **Pledge** says "I commit to this future-verifiable claim, bonded to my OC
  Attest stake." The truth is _forward-looking_ — the outcome is decided later
  and either the pledge is kept (stake intact) or broken (publicly exposed,
  reputation cost).

Use Stamp for archival authorship. Use Pledge for accountability on something
that hasn't happened yet.

## What's the difference between OC Agent and OC Pledge?

Both involve commitments bonded to an OC Attest. The difference is **what's
delegated vs. promised**:

- **Agent** delegates _signing authority_ — a bot can act on your behalf within
  scoped, revocable rights. The bond is collateral against the agent
  misbehaving.
- **Pledge** declares a _future-verifiable proposition_ you'll personally be
  judged against. The bond is collateral against breaking your word.

Agent is "I let this bot do X for me." Pledge is "I swear I will do X."
