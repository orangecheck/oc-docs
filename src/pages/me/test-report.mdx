## Anchor block

ochk.app commits its public records, payouts among them, to Bitcoin through
OpenTimestamps: it sends only the SHA-256 of a record to public calendars, and a
calendar's proof later points into a block
([how anchoring works](/me/api#anchoring)). On the test deployment, anchoring
was switched on for this check on 2026-10-27, with no Nostr relay set, so only
digests left it.

- **All 14 test payouts are anchored in one block**, Bitcoin block 973423,
  `00000000000000000001dbc40e0eedd2f0dd80d6c69d048957305b27b4e95d55`. So are the
  test deployment's payout binding and its one cancelled payout: 16 records in
  all, each read back as anchored after the hourly run at 19:00 UTC on
  2026-10-27 (test log 2026-10-27T19:08:41Z).
- **One of them, checked offline.** Payout
  `05ad976f08bb7fee785a30853ae75fbdfef1e079c70007cb9390fed43dd40ab9`: its id is
  the SHA-256 of its record, and the proof starts from that digest. Following
  the proof reaches block 973423's merkle root, checked against the block header
  served by mempool.space and by blockstream.info, with two verifiers
  (python-opentimestamps 0.4.5's `verify_against_blockheader`, and a second,
  independent parser). The block's time is 2026-10-27 16:24:04 UTC. With one bit
  of the digest flipped, the proof does not reach the root (test log
  2026-10-27T17:15:31Z).
- **Check it yourself.** The proof is
  [`05ad976f….ots`](/test-report/05ad976f08bb7fee785a30853ae75fbdfef1e079c70007cb9390fed43dd40ab9.ots).
  `ots info` on it names the digest and block 973423; any OpenTimestamps
  verifier checks it against the block header.

The test deployment read these records as pending until the anchor run asked the
calendar the proof names. It now does (oc-me-web `0002c5cd`, live 2026-10-27).
On 2026-10-27 production held no records of this kind, so none of production's
had been anchored yet; on 2026-11-10
[me.ochk.io/api/health/deep](https://me.ochk.io/api/health/deep) still read
"none anchored yet".

## Halt-and-restart drill

**2026-10-28, on regtest.** The executor's own code ran unmodified against a
local Fedimint federation (four fedimintd 0.11.4 guardians, an LNv2 gateway on
its own Lightning node) and against the portal's own payout handlers on a local
database. One call was stubbed: fetching an invoice from a Lightning address,
which no regtest server can answer. C04 and C11 proved that hop on mutinynet.
The executor was killed twice on purpose: once after a payment left and before
its receipt was posted, once after it recorded the payment's start and before it
paid.

| #   | What it proves                                                                                                            |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | A payout settles once: one payment, one receipt, the spend counter up by amount plus fee                                  |
| 2   | A halt stops new hand-outs; payouts already handed out finish; the status page reads degraded                             |
| 3   | On resume, each held-back payout is paid exactly once                                                                     |
| 4   | Killed after paying: on restart the executor holds the payout, reports it, and never pays it again                        |
| 5   | The owner marks it paid with the node's proof of payment; it settles, and the action is audited                           |
| 6   | Killed before paying: the owner finds nothing on the node and releases it; it is then paid once                           |
| 7   | The spending ceiling stops a payout that would cross it, and the status page reads it as reached                          |
| 8   | Conservation: 9 payments = 9 settled payouts = 19,832 sats; the node spent 19,963 sats with fees, the same as the counter |

**Run 1** (oc-me-web `8e46fd5b`, 06:09–06:46 UTC) found two faults. In step 6, a
payout the owner released was never paid by the executor that held it. In step
7, the status page read the ceiling as not reached while no payout could fit
under it. Both were fixed that morning.

**Run 2** (oc-me-web `fee8df42`, 06:57–07:23 UTC) passed all 8. The fixes are
live in the portal as oc-me-web `63aeb9e4` (2026-10-28). The executor's part
reaches production with its next update.

## Executor on phoenixd

**2026-10-28, on testnet3, on a machine separate from production.** Production's
executor pays through phoenixd. The executor build then prepared for
production's next update (bundle SHA-256
`3e5f8a7a6bc930bfcf45977f0a2b4a3687e371c2ccb9004b5752a1ad9c1a352a`) was booted
on phoenixd 0.6.0, checked against the image's pinned hash, against a stand-in
for the portal. Its invoices were signed by a key nobody holds, so nothing could
be paid.

- **Boot:** phoenixd and the executor started, and the first report reached the
  portal.
- **Fee cap:** a payout whose routing fee would be 84 sats, over the cap of 50,
  was refused before phoenixd was asked to pay.
- **A payment that fails:** recorded as not sent, nothing reported uncertain,
  and the executor's payment journal written to disk and cleared on the next
  pass.
- **Wrong network, wrong data folder:** the start script refused all 7
  mismatches tried, before phoenixd started.
- **Executor crash:** the start script restarted the executor, and nothing was
  paid twice.

**One finding: phoenixd was not restarted when it died.** Killed on purpose, it
stayed down. The executor kept running and reporting in, and the status page
would still have read it as operational. Nothing was paid wrongly: the portal
holds payouts back while the reports carry no balance, and one already handed
out failed before anything was sent. But payouts would have stopped until
someone restarted the machine.

The fix has three parts. The status page now reads a report with no Lightning
node behind it as degraded; that part has been live in production since
2026-10-28. phoenixd now runs under the same restart supervisor as the executor,
and a shutdown stops the executor before phoenixd. Both are approved, and reach
production with the next executor deploy. The rehearsal ran all three on the
same machine and they passed, including a stop during a payment, where the
payment was held on restart and never paid twice.

**2026-10-31, the build prepared then.** The build that would have replaced
production's executor (bundle SHA-256
`9d061290780af6c7edf45f852f06307debe6aa810b210c5801bab4352de0d109`, oc-me-web
`3eabf682`) keeps the phoenixd fix and adds the executor's half of the privacy
changes under [What operators see](#no-kyc-walk-through): its holds, errors,
logs and payment journal name no payee, and it journals the invoice's hash
before paying. It was rehearsed the same way, on testnet3 on a separate machine,
and passed all four parts:

- **A small payout:** one payment asked of phoenixd, recorded as not sent; the
  journal named no payee and held the invoice's hash before the payment.
- **The executor killed during a payment:** restarted within 5 seconds, the
  payment held, and the hold carries the invoice's hash.
- **A full stop and start on the same data:** the executor stopped before
  phoenixd; the held payout was offered again 26 times and paid 0 more.
- **No payee anywhere:** no invoice in any executor or phoenixd log line,
  journal state or report to the portal.

**2026-11-10, the build approved for production's next update.** oc-me-web
`d0b76fdd` (bundle SHA-256
`75fdbdac2b2d1d09107a88c73abf4112f35a4c44e0c7d4ca1aad0cd5f1accb4f`), built the
same way as the build above and compared with it. On phoenixd it differs in
three ways: a stop that lands while the start script is starting a process stops
the executor as well as phoenixd, a hold's reason is cleaned of anything that
names a payee until nothing more changes, and one error message counts payments
in words. The rest is in the Fedimint drivers, which production's executor does
not run. Nothing it sends to the portal or accepts from it changes. It has not
been rehearsed on phoenixd: the C07 sitting runs it on the test executor first,
stopping and restarting it during a move, and orangecheck deploys it to
production after that.

**Production's executor has not changed.** Its status line on
[me.ochk.io/api/health/deep](https://me.ochk.io/api/health/deep) still reports
the deployment of 2026-10-26, `01M5X2T2VJK57GRY3PY6ZYW3JV` (oc-me-web
`2fc3a6fd`), read 2026-11-10. The portal's side of these changes is live
(oc-me-web `d2e05d6c` to `2b5ad9a4`); the executor's side, phoenixd's restart

## No-KYC walk-through

**2026-10-27.** Every input, write and outbound call on the person's,
integrator's and operator's paths was read in the code, at the then-current
`main` of oc-www (`3796387`), oc-app-web (`7691e53`), oc-me-web (`b8c55f05`),
oc-packages (`d169d906`), oc-guardian-kit (`d41074d`) and oc-docs (`3248cd4`).
Then the paths were walked live on the test deployment by a throwaway account
with a fresh Bitcoin key, every form recorded and nothing submitted, its stored
rows read back and the account purged after. The email-code and Google or GitHub
sign-ins were read in the code, not walked.

**2026-11-11.** What bears on the no-KYC claim (no email, phone or name is
needed to get or use an account, the Bitcoin address is the identity, and
nothing identifying reaches a site outside orangecheck without a grant) was read
again in the code, at the then-current `main` of oc-www (`4ae1e804`), oc-app-web
(`7352ae5d`), oc-me-web (`55ef6c5e`), oc-packages (`17c073a3`), oc-guardian-kit
(`02de1509`) and oc-docs (`82edad09`). It was not walked live again: the test
deployment runs older builds until it is redeployed.

- **A person** signs in with a Bitcoin address and a BIP-322 signature, an email
  and a code, or Google or GitHub. Everything else is optional: a display name
  and a Nostr key (the Profile card says "Both are optional", oc-app-web
  `src/components/oca/user/settings/Profile.tsx:116`), more ways to sign in, a
  backing coin, passkeys. A Lightning address is asked only to cash out. No page
  asks for a legal name, phone, date of birth, ID, selfie, postal address,
  country, tax id or bank details, and no code calls an identity check. An email
  address that takes 20 wrong codes within 24 hours is held: no code for it is
  checked until the oldest of those ages out. Meanwhile signing in or confirming
  a change by email mails a single-use link in place of the code, and every
  other email-code step for that address waits (oc-www
  `src/lib/auth/rate-slots.ts:41`, `src/lib/auth/email-otp.ts:269`). The count
  is per mailbox, with plus tags and Gmail's dots folded, and wrong codes count
  from whoever sends them; the link is what keeps the address's owner able to
  sign in while it is held. Adding an email on ochk.app sends the same sign-in
  code (oc-app-web `src/components/oca/user/identity/LinkedIdentities.tsx` posts
  to `/api/auth/email-otp/start`), so a held address is mailed the link in place
  of the code, and the add form, which takes a code, can add it only once the
  hold ends.
- **An integrator** uses an ordinary account. A project asks for a name, a
  domain and its settings; the domain is proved by DNS or a meta tag. There is
  no billing profile, legal entity, address, country, tax id or payout
  destination: top-ups are Lightning invoices.
- **An operator** needs an invite, an operator key, a signed claim over a recent
  Bitcoin block, signatures on the charter, and a Lightning address to be paid.
  The apply form asks for the questionnaire's answers, and optionally a handle
  and an email.
- **What is published about a person.** Nostr gets only a salted commitment for
  a record that names a person; OpenTimestamps gets a digest. A record's full
  envelope is served to whoever holds its id: that is the receipt model.

**Finding.** The operator questionnaire
([`application-questionnaire.md`](https://github.com/orangecheck/oc-guardian-kit/blob/main/docs/application-questionnaire.md))
asks for a legal name or company name with its jurisdiction, a country and
region, and two references, and says the operator is listed publicly with them.
The portal stores the answers; no code checks or publishes any of it. Whether it
keeps asking is open, with the legal read below. Two documents say more is
published than is. ochk.app's privacy notice names what the questionnaire asks
and says its handle, name, jurisdiction, contact channel and fingerprint are
published in the federation charter (oc-packages `legal/src/content/me.ts:312`),
and the oc-me-v1 charter says each guardian's disclosures appear on
ochk.app/trust (oc-docs `src/pages/federation/oc-me-v1.mdx:70`). The charter
carries the guardians' keys only (oc-me-web
`src/lib/operator/charter.ts:288-290`), and ochk.app/trust lists operator keys
only (oc-app-web `src/pages/trust.tsx:62`).

**What an app receives.** A third-party app (an integrator's project, a site
that opens the sign-in popup, an OC Connect client, a webhook receiver) gets a
person's email address or Bitcoin address only through a scope the person
grants. Granting it, or turning on its renewal, takes a fresh re-proof within 5
minutes: a passkey, or an email code or a Bitcoin signature (oc-me-web
`src/pages/api/me/scope-grants.ts:211` and `:263`,
`src/lib/privacy/identity-reproof.ts:46`). No grant from before that rule is in
force: on 2026-10-28 production held no scope grants at all. On every sign-in
path the popup hands a site outside orangecheck only the account id and did,
with no display name or Nostr key, and the site gets a token bound to its own
origin, with neither address in it (oc-me-web `src/lib/auth/popup-resume.ts:86`,
live since 2026-11-10 in `fad7560d`; oc-www
`src/lib/auth/bound-token.ts:24-32`). orangecheck's own sites are the exception:
the origins on auth-core's family list, which includes me-demo.ochk.io,
orangecheck's demo integration (oc-packages
`auth-core/src/family-origins.ts:23`). The popup also hands them the display
name and Nostr key, and on ochk.io they receive the Bitcoin address and the
sign-in identity without a grant (oc-www `src/pages/api/auth/me.ts:111`).

**Which page opened the popup.** Before it asks for a site's token, the popup
posts a random challenge to the page that opened it, addressed to the origin the
page names, and waits up to 2 seconds for that page to echo it back
(`@orangecheck/me-client` 0.59.0 answers it). orangecheck's own sites are not
asked. Since 2026-11-10 19:19 UTC this reports and does not refuse: the token
request says whether the page answered, and ochk.io logs only the site's origin
and that answer (oc-me-web `src/lib/auth/opener-check.ts:15`, live in
`6b05b930`; oc-www `src/pages/api/auth/audience-token.ts:148`, live in
`b86627d9`). The popup starts refusing a page that does not answer only after
the sites that open it answer on 0.59.0, seven days of that log have been read
site by site, and one sign-in each from Safari on iOS and Chrome on Android has
been seen to answer.

**One session per change on ochk.io.** A request that changes something on
ochk.io, carries more than one ochk.io session cookie and does not name its
tab's session acts for none of them: it is refused, and the answer clears the
other copies the request carried, so the retry carries only the cookie ochk.io
sets at its root (oc-www `src/lib/auth/session.ts:1092-1095`, live since
2026-11-10 in `f7cae1ca`).

**Signing out.** Each site checks a session against its own copy of ochk.io's
list of ended sessions, fetched at most once a minute, so while ochk.io answers
a sign-out reaches every site within a minute. A site with no copy yet, or whose
copy is over 10 minutes old, can't vouch for any session. Then the attest, lock,
vote, stamp, agent and pledge sites, cosign and btc.ochk.io refuse every write
and keep serving reads, so an ended session can still show as signed in there
until ochk.io answers again, unless the copy the site holds lists it. ochk.app,
me.ochk.io, vault, chat and orangecheck's internal tools act for no session
until then (oc-packages `auth-core/src/revocation.ts:319`,
`site-kit/src/auth/session.ts:102`; the
[auth host reference](/api-reference/auth-host#auth-scheme) says what each
answers).

**What deleting an account does.** It does what ochk.app's deletion dialog and
the erasure email say (oc-app-web `f87a2ae`, oc-me-web `1d38acfe`, both live
since 2026-10-28). An admin completes the request, and an alert tells us when
one has waited a day. Completing it closes the account and its sign-in, and
erases the person's data on ochk.io, me.ochk.io, vault, chat and cosign, except
a few records: payment records keep their amounts and times for accounting, and
the audit log and the deletion request stay, without the reason given; each
keeps the account only as a keyed id, or not at all. On cosign, the person's
messages in conversations with other people and the reports they filed stay,
with the account taken off them. Backups roll off within 30 days, and copies
cached by the CDN expire within a day.

The order is me.ochk.io, then vault, chat and cosign, then ochk.io, with a
second pass minutes later (oc-me-web `src/lib/me/account-purge.ts:1-23`). The
purge keys payment records and drops their description
(`src/lib/payment/store.ts:93`), keys the request and drops its reason and the
admin's note (`src/lib/me/deletion-requests-store.ts:223`), drops the person's
webhook retries (`src/lib/developer/webhook-queue.ts:394`), and deletes every
backup row that names the account, in its key or its value
(`src/lib/backup/run-backup.ts:528`), live in oc-me-web `7e1ce4d1`. A nightly
backup that runs while an account is erased checks for the erasure under the
same lock before it writes, so it does not put the account's rows back into the
backups (oc-me-web `4e18989e`, live since 2026-11-05).

**What operators see.** On 2026-10-31 an audit of what orangecheck's own
