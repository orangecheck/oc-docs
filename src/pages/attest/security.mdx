The threat model every orangecheck protocol shares is on
[Ecosystem security](/ecosystem/security). This page covers the
attestation-specific layer on top.

## Report channel

Email **security@ochk.io**. Do not open a public issue for a bug that affects
verifiers. See
[`SECURITY.md`](https://github.com/orangecheck/oc-attest-protocol/blob/main/SECURITY.md#6-reporting)
for the full reporting terms.

## What a valid attestation proves

A verifier that resolves an attestation against live chain state can rely on
three facts:

- **Address control.** The signer held the private key for the address when they
  signed. BIP-322 signature over the canonical message.
- **Sats bonded.** The address held `sats_bonded` sats unspent when the verifier
  resolved the chain, re-derived from current UTXOs, never taken from the
  envelope.
- **Days unspent.** The bonded coin has been confirmed for at least
  `days_unspent` days, also re-derived live.

And one self-asserted fact:

- **Handle ownership.** A claim like `github:alice` or `nostr:npub1…` is the
  address holder's claim. The attestation does not prove it; a verifier may
  check it out of band.

## What an attestation does NOT prove

- **Personhood or uniqueness.** One person can hold many addresses, and many
  addresses can collude. OC Attest raises the cost of sybils; it does not make
  them impossible.
- **Reputation.** Sats × time is an economic signal, not a moral one.
- **Future control.** Custody of the key can change after the signature, by
  theft, sale or a wallet change. Treat an attestation as a point in time.
- **Chain finality.** The holder can spend the coin the moment the attestation
  is published. Re-resolve chain state on every check, and never cache a result
  longer than your tolerance for sybil entry.

## Adversaries

| Adversary                   | What they can do                                  | What stops them                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Anonymous sybil farmer      | Produce many synthetic identities cheaply         | The bond cost scales per identity                                                                                                          |
| Spammer with one wallet     | Sign many messages from one address               | One address is one attestation; the bond does not multiply                                                                                 |
| Stolen-key opportunist      | Sign attestations with a compromised key          | The rightful holder publishes a fresh attestation from a new address                                                                       |
| Compromised hosted verifier | attest.ochk.io answers `ok: true` falsely         | Run the checks yourself with the published packages ([Self-hosting the verifier](/attest/guides/self-hosting)); chain state is open to all |
| Chain-watching deanonymizer | Cluster addresses by spending behaviour           | Out of scope: the attestation publishes the address                                                                                        |
| Block-reorg griefer         | Bond with a transaction that is later reorged out | Verifiers require a confirmation depth                                                                                                     |

OC Attest is not built to defend against coercion (a holder forced to sign once
can be forced again), a compromised publishing platform stripping or replacing
unsigned metadata (re-fetch from at least two relays), or a patient adversary
who bonds for years to look like a long-term holder (cost rises with bond ×
time; size your gate for it).

## What a verifier must do

1. **Re-derive sats and days** from current chain state. Do not trust
   `sats_bonded` or `days_unspent` in the envelope.
2. **Check the BIP-322 signature** against the exact canonical message bytes.
   See [Canonical message](/ecosystem/canonical-message).
3. **Check the audience** when your gate scopes proofs to one origin, and drop
   attestations issued for a different site.
4. **Require a confirmation depth** before counting `days_unspent`. Six is the
   default in `@orangecheck/sdk` (`BOND_MIN_CONFIRMATIONS`); high-stakes gates
   should require more.
5. **Apply your freshness window.** The signature has no expiry of its own.
6. **Take the subject from the signed message.** Relay tags and the envelope's
   `address`, `identities`, `issued_at` and `expires_at` are not signed. An
   attestation answers a query only if its canonical message states the id,
   address or identity asked for.
7. **Hold one bond to one identity per protocol.** If the address also binds a
   different identity on the same protocol, refuse with `stake_shared`.

The exact algorithm is on [Verification](/attest/verification).

## Privacy

Every attestation is public: it reveals the address, the bonded coin and any
claimed handles. There is no zero-knowledge mode. Use a different address per
context to keep gates from seeing each other's attestations; on-chain analysis
can still cluster them.
