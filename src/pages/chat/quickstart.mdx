# Quickstart

Five minutes to your first end-to-end-encrypted message. No app install —
[chat.ochk.io](https://chat.ochk.io) runs in the browser.

## 1. Sign in

Open [chat.ochk.io](https://chat.ochk.io) and sign in with your orangecheck
account on the [auth host](/api-reference/auth-host): email, Google, GitHub or a
Bitcoin wallet. The account you sign in with is your inbox, and its id is a
`did:oc`.

The first time you open the app on a device, **Create device & open inbox**
generates an **X25519 device key**. ochk.io vouches for it, and the browser
publishes a [kind-30078 device record](/lock/device-keys) for your account
([session devices](/chat/spec#session-devices-86)). No wallet signature is
needed. From here, sending and receiving are zero-click.

Binding a Bitcoin address is optional: Settings › Identity › Bitcoin address,
one [BIP-322](/ecosystem/bip322) signature per device. A bound address lets
people reach you at it, and you need one to claim a handle or charge postage.

> Your inbox pubkey is `HKDF(device_sk)` — derived from the device key, not
> chosen. Publishing a device record _is_ creating an inbox; there is no second
> ceremony.

## 2. Send your first message (speak-now)

1. Click **New** and enter the recipient's `did:oc`, **Bitcoin address** or
   `@handle`.
2. The app fetches their kind-30078 device records from Nostr. For a Bitcoin
   address it **verifies the BIP-322 binding offline**; for a `did:oc` it checks
   ochk.io's signed vouch, and the message details read "via ochk.io, not
   offline-verifiable". A malicious relay cannot forge either.
3. Type and send. The app seals an OC Lock envelope to the recipient's device
   key, gift-wraps it (NIP-59, kind-1059) with a throwaway Schnorr key, and
   publishes it.
4. The relay sees an ephemeral pubkey, the recipient's inbox pubkey, a
   minute-rounded timestamp, and an opaque blob — **not who sent it, what it
   says, or which conversation it belongs to.**

No wallet popup. The send loop is sub-second.

### Note to self

Start a thread with **your own** address: the client seals to your own device
key, the subscription loops the gift-wrap back, and it lands in a thread labeled
"note to self." Every device you have registered under that address receives it
— so this doubles as an end-to-end-encrypted cross-device clipboard.

## 3. Let strangers reach you (pay-to-reach)

By default anyone can reach your inbox. Under Settings › Inbox › "Who can reach
you", **Contacts only** or **Bitcoin floor** moves a first message from someone
who is **not** an existing contact into a separate **Requests** surface, not
your main inbox — it is delivered and decrypted, never dropped. To price that
attention instead of merely filtering it, set a **postage price** under Settings
› Inbox › Pay-to-reach: a sats floor and a Lightning Address resolving to a
wallet **you** control. The price is published in your
[directory listing](/chat/directory), so it needs a listed `@handle` first. Once
it is set, a stranger's first message without valid postage lands in Requests.

A stranger's wallet then fetches an invoice **directly from your endpoint**,
pays it, and attaches the preimage to their first message. Your client verifies
`SHA-256(preimage) == payment_hash` offline and that the payment is bound to you
and this message. Valid → inbox. **OC is never in the payment path and collects
nothing.** Full mechanics in [Postage](/chat/postage).

## 4. Send a message that opens in the future (seal-til-block)

Compose a message and pick a future **Bitcoin block height**. The app locks the
body under a reveal secret and timelock-encrypts that secret to a **named
beacon** (drand quicknet by default), then sends it. The recipient receives the
ciphertext immediately but holds no key until the chain passes the height.

The compose flow **forces you to acknowledge** the trust posture before you
send:

> The named beacon can release early if its threshold colludes, and the seal is
> permanently bricked if the beacon disappears. A v0 seal is **beacon-enforced,
> not trustless.** The reference build caps the horizon at ~30 days.

See [Seal-til-block](/chat/seal) for the beacon model, the hard chain gate, and
the dead-man's-switch (standing delivery) composition.

## 5. Be findable by a handle (optional)

By default you are **invisible** — discoverable only by someone who already
knows your `did:oc` or a Bitcoin address you have bound. If you opt in, you can
publish a revocable [directory listing](/chat/directory) so people can find you
by a human-readable `@handle`. Claiming a handle requires a bound Bitcoin
address that clears a **UTXO-age floor** (funded + aged on-chain), which prices
out squatting. Opt-in surfaces the deanonymization trade-off plainly; revocation
is forward-effective only.

## What you get on the free tier

Everything above works on the **free tier**: all three send modes, the opt-in
directory, public [channels](/chat/channels), and a best-effort **durable
inbox** so a message sent while you are offline still arrives. Durability of
basic delivery is never the paywall. The plan's paid **Personal** rung is 1,000
sats / 30 days, prepaid over Lightning, with no auto-renew. Its checkout is not
open, so Personal cannot be bought today.
