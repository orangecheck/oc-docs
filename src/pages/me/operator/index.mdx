# ochk.app · Operator Program

**Run a guardian. Earn sats. Hold the key.**

In the design, an orangecheck federation is run by independent **guardians**:
one operator key per guardian, each guardian threshold-signing federation
actions with its peers. orangecheck recruits, vets and pays guardians. Some of
what an operator does on ochk.app needs its own operator key's signature as well
as a signed-in session, and the rest takes the session alone;
[charter §4](/federation/oc-me-v1#4-orangechecks-role) lists which.

**The program is not open.** While the system is in testing, orangecheck holds
every key that can move funds, and there are no guardian seats yet.
[ochk.app/custody](https://ochk.app/custody) shows who holds what today. The
operator pages on ochk.app open only to accounts the program lets in; to anyone
else the operator routes on me.ochk.io answer `403`. This page describes how the
program works.

Two paths into it:

- **[The portal at ochk.app](https://ochk.app/me/operator)** — sign in, make or
  import an operator key, fill the application form, sign and submit in the
  browser. The lifecycle (key custody, application, charter ratification,
  ceremonies, incident publishing) runs in the portal without a CLI.
- **[The kit](https://github.com/orangecheck/oc-guardian-kit)** —
  `oc-guardian init` on a server you control, fill the questionnaire, run
  `oc-guardian apply prepare`, and email `application.json` with your filled-in
  questionnaire to apply@ochk.io. The kit signs everything the portal signs but
  two: claiming a seat and voting on an amendment are signed only in the portal
  today. And ochk.app has no upload yet for a charter signature the kit signs,
  so only a key held in the portal can ratify a charter today. The envelopes are
  the same, and so is the review queue.

## Lifecycle

| Stage           | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Where                                                                      |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **identity**    | Generate (or import) an Ed25519 operator keypair. | **application** | Fill the questionnaire (handle, contact, hardware setup, motivation, etc.). `oc-guardian apply prepare` hashes the answers and signs a `program-apply` envelope into `application.json`; email it to apply@ochk.io with the questionnaire attached, since the envelope carries the answers' hash, not the answers. | Kit + email                                                                |
| **review**      | OC team verifies the envelope signature, reads the questionnaire, and decides. Reviewer signs an `program-accept` envelope on accept; you verify it locally before trusting.                                                                                                                                       | Reviewer side                                                              |
| **federation**  | Once you are accepted, a federation's creator holds a seat for you (on the federations orangecheck creates, that is orangecheck), you claim it with your operator key, and you ratify the federation's charter: a `charter-sign` envelope under your operator key. The ratification is your join.                  | [ochk.app/me/operator/ceremonies](https://ochk.app/me/operator/ceremonies) |
| **operate**     | Run the `oc-guardian` binary on hardware you control or hardware OC provisions on your behalf. Publish incidents (`alert-publish`), participate in ceremonies (`charter-sign`), claim payouts (`payouts-claim`).                                                                                                   | Portal + binary                                                            |

## Two ways to run the guardian binary

The federation cannot distinguish between these. Pick whichever fits.

### Option A · bring your own hardware

You own the host. Pull the cosign-verified Docker image and run
`oc-guardian fedimintd run`. Its settings come from the environment: your
operator id and pubkey, the federation, the bitcoin network, a chain source and
the public host name. Peers reach it on port 9000 (P2P) and clients on port 9001
(API). Full runbook: [/me/operator/runbook](/me/operator/runbook).

### Option B · let OC provision for you

Opt into hosted hardware on the dashboard. orangecheck runs the signed image on
one hosting provider, in a region you choose, and the portal can restart, stop
or destroy the machine. Your operator key never touches the host: charter
ratifications, payout claims and exits are still signed in your browser or your
kit. The guardian's `fedimintd` does hold its own key share on that machine, and
it signs liveness reports with a key orangecheck made, which counts for your
seat only while you delegate to it. A hosted guardian is only as independent as
orangecheck, which runs the machine. Where that matters, run it on hardware you
control. Once your federation has run its key ceremony, the machine's volume
holds a key share your operator mnemonic cannot restore, so orangecheck can
decommission it, while the federation is not retired, only with a typed reason
written to its audit log first.

## Architectural invariants

What the program is built to hold, and where each falls short today.

1. **Operator-held keys.** orangecheck's servers never receive your operator
   key. Where the key lives decides who else can reach it. The kit keeps it in
   the OS keychain or a key file, outside any page. The portal keeps it in
   ochk.app's browser storage, where the script ochk.app serves can read it, and
   orangecheck deploys that script. For a key no page can read, keep it in the
   kit.
2. **Bypass parity.** The kit signs everything the portal signs but two, each
   step in
   [BYPASS.md](https://github.com/orangecheck/oc-guardian-kit/blob/main/BYPASS.md):
   claiming a seat and voting on an amendment are signed only in the portal
   today. The kit signs without the portal, and what it signs is uploaded on
   ochk.app, signed in, except a charter signature, which ochk.app has no upload
   for yet. At the federation, a guardian run from the kit looks the same as one
   run from the portal.
3. **Public commitments.** A charter ratification, incident or attestation made
   now is operator-signed and verifiable offline, so OC cannot forge one. Two
   kinds of row fall short. One kept without its envelope cannot be checked
   again: the one charter signature on record, over the superseded v1.0.0, reads
   `offline_verifiable: false`, and incidents recorded before 2026-11-08 kept
   none. And a hosted seat's liveness reports are signed with a key orangecheck
   made (Option B above).
4. **Funds sit with the federation.** In a federation, the guardians hold the
   bitcoin collectively, and orangecheck's executor holds each user's claim on
   it, as ecash it can spend, until it pays them out
   ([ochk.app/custody](https://ochk.app/custody)). Today there are no guardian
   seats: while the system is in testing, orangecheck holds every key that can
   move funds, and the sats users earn.
